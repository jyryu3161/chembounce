# Training-inventory evidence audit

This is a post hoc provenance audit, not a held-out performance claim. No complete, checkpoint-linked molecular training inventory was established for the exact SynLlama or ReactionT5 checkpoint used in the current campaigns. This differs from saying that no public training datasets exist: an author-published ZINC inventory is available, but its contents were not downloaded or matched to the exact base-checkpoint revision used here. The benchmark's scaffold, publication-year and target partitions separate its own records; they do not by themselves establish independence from pretrained-model exposure.

## SynLlama-1B-2M-91rxns

The [author release, Figshare v1](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.28719125.v1) contains the campaign's checkpoint and a small train-labelled evaluation sample in the same archive. The retained archive is 7,773,956,257 bytes; SHA256 `d46ceb49c3e2949c6e65386187d07c8866852de93f20d710daebb57597acebed`. The checkpoint weight SHA256 is `4c85cf6e568b66f299cf235a3196a326fc7bebd41b8fb90be4c018ed5ea79781`.

The [final paper's Table 1 footnote](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12670306/) describes selecting 1,000 molecular inputs from each of its training, testing and ChEMBL sources for RXN 1 model evaluation. The archive member `synllama-data/inference/smiles/syn-planning/1k_train_91rxns.smi` is consistent with that train-labelled evaluation scope. The upstream [inference guide at revision 57a98b6](https://github.com/THGLab/SynLlama/blob/57a98b658eae8ba4f6f1d04aa99c4f91da566fb0/assets/docs/inference_guide.md) associates RXN 1 with the 91-template checkpoint. [Supporting Table S1](https://ndownloader.figshare.com/files/58041987) explicitly describes the two 1B/2M models and 1,000 evaluation inputs generated using their corresponding reaction-template sets. This strengthens the publication-level association but supplies no exact corpus-to-checkpoint row mapping.

This establishes **an upstream train-labelled evaluation sample**, not verified membership in the exact 2M checkpoint's fine-tuning set. No released row identifiers, dataset digest, split manifest or exact checkpoint-to-corpus mapping was found. The repository does not reference the `1k_train_91rxns.smi` filename in an executable checkpoint-specific evaluation command. The archive's Table 1 result filenames even contain `1k_train_115rxns` inside the `91rxns` result directory; filenames alone cannot resolve that ambiguity. No result pickle was deserialized. The SMILES sample is also not a complete inventory of reactants, intermediates and products appearing in training-route outputs.

The [retraining guide](https://github.com/THGLab/SynLlama/blob/57a98b658eae8ba4f6f1d04aa99c4f91da566fb0/assets/docs/retraining_guide.md) instructs users to obtain Enamine building blocks and generate new pathway JSONL data. The [SFT configuration](https://github.com/THGLab/SynLlama/blob/57a98b658eae8ba4f6f1d04aa99c4f91da566fb0/synllama/llm/sft/synllama_sft.yml) contains a dataset-path placeholder. Those resources provide a recipe, not the original two-million-route inventory. The archive listing contains 554 entries, including models, reconstruction assets, evaluation inputs and reported results, but no identified full fine-tuning JSONL or corpus manifest. Building-block catalogs and reaction matrices do not enumerate the checkpoint's selected training products/routes. The general Llama pretraining inventory is also not established.

Licenses must remain separate. Figshare metadata declares MIT for its release; the inspected [repository LICENSE](https://github.com/THGLab/SynLlama/blob/57a98b658eae8ba4f6f1d04aa99c4f91da566fb0/LICENSE) grants educational, research and not-for-profit software/documentation use, despite the paper describing MIT. The retraining guide separately says Enamine building blocks require obtaining access; the Llama base model has its own terms. These statements do not establish unrestricted redistribution rights for the sample's underlying molecular inventory. The extracted sample and molecule-bearing overlap records remain ignored local review artifacts. Share aggregate counts, hashes and retrieval procedures unless dataset redistribution terms are resolved.

## ReactionT5v2-forward and CompoundT5

The campaign uses [ReactionT5v2-forward revision 9331140](https://huggingface.co/sagawa/ReactionT5v2-forward/tree/933114058cb2604dc1bf536dbebdfcefbe83d4fc), with weight SHA256 `69c23ca364e63e0e2be22d114652aad9f3df4e161d43936eee0dc3ce74a87fb7`. Its [pinned model card](https://huggingface.co/sagawa/ReactionT5v2-forward/blob/933114058cb2604dc1bf536dbebdfcefbe83d4fc/README.md) names ORD training, USPTO-MIT test-set exclusion and an **unpinned** `sagawa/CompoundT5` base. This is not the separately named USPTO-MIT-fine-tuned checkpoint.

The author links [ORD.zip](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1JozA2OlByfZ-ILt5H5YrTjLJvSvD8xdL/view); the retained Drive preview reports 233,136,954 bytes. Its contents were not downloaded, inspected or verified against the exact checkpoint. The [forward training code](https://github.com/sagawatatsuya/ReactionT5v2/blob/76eb08068e10fe255cae5d563a91e1c1e9abac54/task_forward/train.py) performs parsing, canonicalization, duplicate handling and exclusion against USPTO test reactions before training. That inspected code revision is not pinned by the checkpoint card and is a reproduction recipe, not an original execution trace. A complete molecular audit would need the actual post-filter rows and all molecular roles, not only reaction products. The retained model metadata and safely inspected pickle opcodes in `training_args.bin` did not provide an exact training-data digest. No pickle was executed.

An author-published [ZINC-canonicalized inventory at revision 5497e79](https://huggingface.co/datasets/sagawa/ZINC-canonicalized/tree/5497e797c551617bc1d94a859e4f3429f3d0b32d) **does exist**. Its declared schema is one `smiles` string per row: 20,693,269 train rows and 2,299,253 validation rows. Three train Parquet files total 523,819,107 bytes; including validation gives 582,020,457 bytes. File LFS SHA256 values, split counts and schema were retained in `zinc-canonicalized-metadata.json` and `zinc-canonicalized-dataset-infos.json`; the data shards were not downloaded. The [CompoundT5 model card](https://huggingface.co/sagawa/CompoundT5/blob/c90452eddf33334da4201f46683b5081b5d7d0dd/README.md) links this dataset. This makes a future audit against that specific public dataset feasible; it does not prove that the exact forward checkpoint used that exact base revision or all those rows. The [ReactionT5 paper](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13321-025-01075-4) describes both compound and reaction pretraining stages, so auditing only one stage would remain incomplete.

The ReactionT5 repository and model card declare MIT; the ZINC dataset card declares Apache 2.0. Those declarations do not automatically settle underlying ZINC or ORD record terms. There is no current complete, exact-checkpoint overlap pass for ReactionT5. Public inventory availability, successful retrieval, verified row content and verified checkpoint association are separate statuses.

No evaluation-input identity failed at either level. Repeated query/control appearances and the evaluated/rescored copies of returned candidates explain those counts; they are not independent compounds or observations.

These results establish only nonmembership in the assessed portion of this small, upstream-labelled evaluation sample at the specified identity levels. They do **not** establish absence from the exact checkpoint's training data, absence of scaffold-related analogues, or clean pretrained-model holdout status. The sample-to-checkpoint association remains unverified, and its 72 unassessed family identities must not be counted as family nonmatches.
